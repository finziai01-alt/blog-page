How to track a client who pays in instalments — without marking them fully paid.

The invoice says ₹40,000. The client has paid ₹15,000 twice. Somewhere in your books, that invoice is either honestly open or quietly wrong. Here is how to keep it honest.

The two ways this goes wrong

Most accounting tools give an invoice one flag: paid, or unpaid. Reality is a spectrum. A client pays in parts — an advance, then two instalments, then a final amount weeks later. With a binary flag you face two bad options: keep the invoice "unpaid" for weeks while money has actually arrived, or click it "paid" when the last instalment lands and lose the record that the earlier ones were partial. Either way, the books stop describing what happened.

The honest representation is simple: record every instalment against the invoice, and keep the remaining balance visible. Status reads Partial Payment until the balance is exactly zero.

The four-step method

  1. Record each instalment against its invoice. Not as a generic "receipt" floating in the ledger — applied to the specific invoice, so the invoice itself tells the story.
  2. Keep the balance visible. Received, outstanding, total — shown together. Anyone reading the entry sees the truth without reconstructing it from the bank.
  3. Label it Partial Payment until zero. The status changes only when the balance reaches zero. Not when a big chunk arrives. Not when you get bored of seeing it open.
  4. Reconcile against the bank statement. When the statement arrives, the instalments you recorded should match the credits in the bank. Anything unmatched is a question to answer, not a number to ignore.

What to demand from your software

  • Part-payments applied per invoice, with the balance recalculated each time
  • A status that says Partial Payment while money is outstanding
  • Bank matching that finds instalments automatically and stacks them
  • Approval before any payment action changes your books
  • A view your client can understand — because "you still owe ₹10,000" is easier to hear when the math is on screen

Common questions

What is a partial payment in accounting?

A payment that covers only part of an invoice. The invoice stays open for the remaining balance, and the account shows what was received and what is still owed.

Why is marking an invoice paid early dangerous?

It overstates revenue and hides money that is still owed. The receivable disappears from view until someone notices the bank balance doesn't match the books.

How should instalments be shown to a client?

As a stack against the invoice: each instalment recorded, the balance visible, and a status of Partial Payment until the balance reaches zero. Honest books make chasing the remainder easier, not harder.
