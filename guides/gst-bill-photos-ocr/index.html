How to read GST breakup from bill photos, automatically.

A photo of a bill is not data — until something reads it. Here is what automatic GST reading actually does, where it fails, and why the verification step is the part that matters.

What's actually on a GST bill

Beneath the vendor name and the item lines sits the part your books need: the GSTIN of each party, the invoice number and date, HSN or SAC codes, taxable value, and the tax split — CGST and SGST for intra-state supply, IGST for inter-state — adding up to the grand total. When that breakup is typed by hand, every digit is a chance to transpose a number, and a wrong GST figure doesn't stay small: it flows into your returns.

The four-step workflow

  1. Photograph the bill clearly. Flat, well-lit, all four corners visible. Phone photos of printed bills are the normal input — this is not a "scan it perfectly" workflow.
  2. Read every field, including the tax split. Vendor, GSTIN, invoice number, date, HSN codes, taxable value, CGST, SGST, IGST and the grand total — read from the document itself. GST is taken exactly as printed.
  3. Show each field beside the original. Extracted fields appear next to the photo, so verification is a glance at the original, not a second transcription.
  4. Flag calculated totals. If the bill doesn't print a total, the calculated figure is clearly marked for you to verify before anything is saved. A number you can't see on the document should never enter your books silently.

Where it fails — honestly

  • Handwriting: readable when neat, unreliable when not. Treat as draft.
  • Glare, creases, cuts: the photo quality sets the ceiling. Re-shoot instead of arguing with the output.
  • Non-standard layouts: unusual formats exist. When a field looks wrong, it's cheaper to check the photo than to trust the guess.
  • The point of approval: every one of these failure modes is handled by the same control — fields shown beside the original, nothing saved until a person approves it.

Common questions

Can software read GST breakup from a photo?

Yes — for clear photos of printed bills, vendor name, GSTIN, invoice number, date, taxable value and the CGST/SGST/IGST split are read automatically. The photo must be flat, lit and fully in frame.

What does automatic GST reading miss?

Handwriting, heavy creases, glare, badly cropped photos and bills with non-standard layouts. Treat those as drafts to verify rather than facts to trust blindly.

How do I know the GST numbers are right?

By looking at them. Each field is shown beside the original document, so checking the split against the photo takes seconds. Calculated totals — where the bill didn't print one — are flagged explicitly.
